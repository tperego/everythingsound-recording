Console Signal Flow Trainer

An SSL AWS 900 laid out like the real desk: eight channel strips in the desk's top‑to‑bottom order, a centre section with the oscillator, talkback, monitor, bus compressor, Dynamics 1 and 2, track bus and cue/FX masters, foldbacks and stereo returns, and one master fader for the REC or MIX bus. Plus a normalled patchbay, an eight‑track DAW, an outboard reverb and two outboard compressors. Press buttons, turn knobs, patch cables; the schematic, the outputs and the audio follow.

Each track records from the DAW input you pick under Input. On the patchbay, each channel's direct out (post‑fader as standard) is normalled to the DAW input with the same number, so channel n reaches DAW input n as soon as its fader is up. The input menu picks which of those you record. Patch a track bus over a DAW input when you want to combine several channels on one track. Each track plays back to the DAW output you pick under Output, and DAW output n is normalled to channel n's line input, so the output picks which console channel the track returns on. An armed track (REC) or one with IN pressed plays its input instead of the recording. A take is one pass of the four‑bar loop and replaces what was on the track.

FX send 1 is normalled to the reverb input, and the reverb output to echo return 1. Patch other sources in on the patchbay (for example a channel direct out, a track bus or an insert send). With MIX at 100% the unit outputs only the reverb, the usual setting on an aux send. Below 100% the dry signal comes through as well.

Two outboard compressors, COMP 1 and COMP 2. Nothing is normalled to them: patch each one on the patchbay, usually channel insert send to Comp in and Comp out to the channel insert return, with INS pressed on that channel. You can also put one across the MIX or REC bus insert, or feed it from a track bus. On the console itself there are two more compressors, Dynamics 1 and 2 in the centre section, which any channel can pick with its DYNAMICS 1 or 2 button, and the MIX bus has the bus compressor.

Signal flow exam (formative)

Start the exam to zero the console and the studio. One condenser mic is waiting in the live room on wall box 1 (Input X = channel 1). Bring the DAW return back on channel 5, and the click on any other free channel. Each block scores 1 (done right), 0.5 (partly done) or 0 (fundamental error, such as 48V switched on after the gain, or a mic unplugged with 48V on). Scores lock in once earned, so you can move on; a fundamental error sets the block to 0. Only General attitude is left to the tutor; on the standalone desk the tutor can click its score.

Teaching model laid out like the SSL AWS 900 class diagrams: each strip runs meter, routing, dynamics, input, EQ, cue/FX, REC/MIX, SEL/SOLO/CUT, fader; the centre section has the oscillator, talkback, monitor, bus compressor, Dynamics 1 and 2, track bus and cue/FX masters, foldbacks and stereo returns; one master fader serves the REC or MIX bus. Simplified: eight channels, mono patch points, no surround, no gate/expander, approximate levels and EQ curves. Always confirm details on the real console.
